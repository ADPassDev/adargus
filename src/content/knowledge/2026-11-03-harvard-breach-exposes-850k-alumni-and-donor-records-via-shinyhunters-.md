Harvard University disclosed a data breach affecting its alumni and fundraising systems, with roughly 851,000 unique email addresses exposed alongside detailed profile data on donors, alumni, parents, and some faculty, staff, and students. Compromised fields include phone numbers, home and business addresses, event attendance, donation history, and other biographical details. The incident has been linked to the ShinyHunters group and a "pay or leak" extortion scheme.

While this breach does not appear to involve passwords or direct credential theft, the volume of verified email addresses and rich personal context creates fertile ground for targeted phishing and social engineering. Attackers armed with donation histories and relationship details can craft highly convincing lures against a wealthy, high-profile population.

What to take away: contact and profile data from fundraising CRMs is a potent phishing accelerant. Organizations should treat alumni/donor databases as sensitive attack-enabling assets and ensure affected individuals are warned to watch for tailored impersonation and credential-harvesting attempts.
