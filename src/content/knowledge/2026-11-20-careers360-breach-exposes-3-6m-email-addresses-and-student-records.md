In November 2026, a dataset allegedly stolen from Careers360, an Indian education and careers platform, was posted to a public hacking forum. The 22 million records contained 3.6 million unique email addresses along with names, phone numbers, genders, caste categories, and in most cases students' entrance exam results including ranks and marks.

The exposure of email addresses, phone numbers, and sensitive demographic data creates meaningful risk for credential-based attacks. Aggregated personal information like this fuels targeted phishing and social engineering campaigns, and reused credentials from such breaches frequently enable account takeover and downstream access to corporate identity systems.

What to take away: Breaches of consumer platforms feed the credential-stuffing ecosystem that threatens enterprise identity infrastructure. Organizations should monitor for exposed employee emails, enforce MFA, and screen against known-compromised credential lists.
