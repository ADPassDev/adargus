In August 2026, food and animal safety organisation Neogen was hit by a ShinyHunters "pay or leak" extortion attempt. After the demand, the group published data it attributed to Neogen, comprising roughly 436,000 unique email addresses drawn from mailing lists, employee records, and other corporate contacts.

While the exposed data is largely contact-oriented rather than credentials, leaked employee records and email addresses are valuable raw material for phishing, credential-stuffing, and targeted social engineering against identity systems. Attackers frequently mine such dumps to map an organisation's staff and craft convincing lures aimed at harvesting corporate logins.

What to take away: treat exposed employee email lists as an elevated phishing and account-takeover risk — reinforce MFA, monitor for suspicious authentication attempts against affected accounts, and alert staff to likely targeted lures referencing this breach.
